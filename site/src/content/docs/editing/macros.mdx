Press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>U</kbd>, make an edit, and press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>U</kbd> again. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>J</kbd> replays it.

## Repeat an edit on many lines

<Steps>

1. Put the cursor at the start of the first line.
2. Press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>U</kbd>. The status line shows **● REC**.
3. Make the edit, and end on the next line: for example <kbd>End</kbd>, type `;`, <kbd>↓</kbd>, <kbd>Home</kbd>.
4. Press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>U</kbd> to stop. The status line says how many keys were recorded.
5. Press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>J</kbd> once per line.

</Steps>

| Key | Typed command | Does |
|---|---|---|
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>U</kbd> | `:record` | Start or stop recording |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>J</kbd> | `:replay` | Replay the last macro |

## Good to know

- A macro records keys, not mouse clicks, and replays them through the same key handling, so find, quick open and commands work inside it.
- Skink keeps one macro, until you record another or quit.
- For the same change at many places at once, [multiple cursors](/editing/multiple-cursors/) are often quicker.
- Started recording by accident? <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>U</kbd> stops it.
